A slow guide to loose-leaf tea

The Quiet Kettle is a small guide for people who want to drink better tea without turning it into a hobby that takes over their kitchen. Every page here is short, practical and written to be read with a cup already in hand.

All true tea comes from one plant, Camellia sinensis. The differences between a grassy green, a honeyed oolong and a malty black tea come almost entirely from what happens after the leaf is picked: how long it is left to wither, whether it is rolled or bruised, and how much it is allowed to oxidise before it is dried.

Where to start

  • Green tea — fresh, vegetal, and the easiest to over-brew.
  • Black tea — fully oxidised, forgiving, and good with milk.
  • Oolong — the wide middle ground, from floral to roasted.
  • White tea — the least processed, soft and sweet.

Then get the basics right

Most disappointing cups come from three things: water that is too hot, leaves left in too long, and tea that has gone stale in the cupboard. The brewing guide, teaware notes and storage page cover each of those in a few minutes of reading.
