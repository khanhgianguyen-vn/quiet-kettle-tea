Oolong tea

Oolong is partly oxidised — anywhere from about 10 to 80 percent — which makes it the most varied of all tea families. Two oolongs can taste less alike than a green and a black.

Light, rolled oolongs

Tightly rolled into small balls, teas like Tieguanyin and high-mountain Taiwanese oolongs are floral, creamy and green-leaning. They unfurl dramatically in the pot.

Dark, roasted oolongs

Long-leaf teas such as Wuyi rock teas (Da Hong Pao) are more oxidised and charcoal-roasted, with notes of stone fruit, caramel and minerals.

Brewing

Use 85–95 °C water. Oolong rewards short, repeated infusions: try 30–45 seconds in a small pot and keep going for six or more rounds, adding a few seconds each time.
