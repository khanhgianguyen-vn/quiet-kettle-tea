White tea

White tea is simply picked, withered and dried. With no rolling or firing, it keeps a soft, sweet, hay-like character and a pale golden liquor.

Main grades

  • Silver Needle (Baihao Yinzhen) — unopened buds only, delicate and honeyed.
  • White Peony (Bai Mudan) — a bud with one or two leaves, fuller and fruitier.
  • Shou Mei — later-season leaves, earthier and more robust.

Aged white tea

Pressed white tea cakes are often aged for years, developing notes of dried dates and medicinal herbs. Aged white is one of the few teas that improves with time.

Brewing

Use 80–90 °C water and a generous amount of leaf. Two to four minutes is a good start; white tea rarely turns bitter.
