Black tea

Black tea is fully oxidised: the leaves are withered, rolled to break their cell walls, and left to darken before drying. That process builds the deep colour and the malty, sometimes fruity flavour most people picture when they hear the word "tea".

Regions worth knowing

  • Assam — bold and malty, the backbone of most breakfast blends.
  • Darjeeling — lighter and muscatel, often closer to an oolong in character.
  • Ceylon — bright and brisk, good iced.
  • Keemun — smooth, with a gentle cocoa and smoke note.

Brewing

Black tea takes water just off the boil (90–98 °C) and three to five minutes. It is the most forgiving family: a little too long usually just means a stronger cup, which milk can soften.
