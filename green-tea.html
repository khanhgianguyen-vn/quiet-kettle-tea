Green tea

Green tea is heated soon after picking to stop oxidation, which keeps the leaf green and the flavour fresh. How that heat is applied splits green tea into two broad styles.

Pan-fired (Chinese style)

Leaves are tossed in a hot wok or drum. The result tends to be nutty, toasty and gentle — think Dragon Well (Longjing) with its flat, sword-shaped leaves and chestnut sweetness.

Steamed (Japanese style)

Leaves are steamed for anywhere from a few seconds to a couple of minutes. Steamed teas such as sencha are brighter, more marine and more vivid in colour. Longer steaming (fukamushi) gives a cloudier, fuller cup.

Brewing it well

Use water around 70–80 °C and start with one to two minutes. If the cup turns bitter, lower the temperature before you shorten the time. Good green tea will happily give three or four infusions.
